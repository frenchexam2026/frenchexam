Leaving Cert French Journal Intime

The tenses, feelings and structure that make a diary entry stand out, with a Higher Level sample entry you can learn from.

Written exam guide Opinion piece Journal intime Letters & emails

A journal intime is about how you felt, not just what happened. The strongest entries tell a clear story, react to it honestly and move confidently between past, present and future. A neutral report of events misses the point of the task.

What a strong diary entry includes

  1. A date and an opening. Cher journal, or an exclamation such as Quelle journée ! sets the informal tone straight away.
  2. What happened, in the past. Use the passé composé for events and the imparfait for background and feelings at the time.
  3. How you feel now. React in the present tense. This is the heart of the task.
  4. What happens next. A plan or resolution in the future or conditional shows range, and si clauses show it best.
  5. A natural sign-off. Bon, il est tard… or À demain, rounds it off.

Phrases for feelings and reactions

  • Quelle journée !What a day!
  • Je n’arrive pas à y croire !I can’t believe it!
  • J’étais aux anges.I was over the moon.
  • J’en ai marre.I’m fed up.
  • J’ai le cafard.I’m feeling down.
  • Ça m’a fait chaud au cœur.It warmed my heart.
  • Ce qui m’énerve le plus, c’est que…What annoys me most is that…
  • Si j’avais su, j’aurais…If I had known, I would have…
  • Désormais, je vais…From now on, I’m going to…

Higher Level sample answer

Journal intime

« Vous venez de recevoir les résultats de vos examens blancs. Ils sont décevants. Écrivez une page de journal intime. »

You have just received your mock exam results. They are disappointing. Write a diary entry.

Sample answer

Mardi 3 février
Cher journal,

Quelle journée horrible ! Ce matin, le prof principal nous a rendu les résultats des examens blancs et j’ai complètement raté les maths. Quand j’ai vu la note, j’ai eu envie de pleurer. Pourtant, j’avais révisé pendant tout le week-end ! Ce qui m’énerve le plus, c’est que mes parents vont être déçus. Si j’avais commencé plus tôt, j’aurais sans doute eu une meilleure note. Mais bon, ce n’est pas la fin du monde. Dès demain, je vais demander de l’aide à ma prof et je ferai un planning de révision. Il faut que je reste positive. Allez, au lit !

À demain,

Count the tenses: passé composé, imparfait, plus-que-parfait, present, futur proche, futur simple, conditionnel passé and a subjunctive, all in a natural voice. That range is what lifts an entry to H1.

Common journal intime mistakes

  • Reporting events without saying how you felt about them.
  • Using only the passé composé, with no imparfait for background or feelings.
  • Forgetting the part of the task that asks what you’ll do next.
  • Slipping into a formal tone. You’re writing to yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

What tenses should I use in a Leaving Cert French journal intime?

Use the passé composé for what happened and the imparfait for background and how you felt at the time, the present for how you feel now, and the future or conditional for what you’ll do next. A si clause such as « Si j’avais su, j’aurais… » is an easy way to show range.

How do I start a journal intime in French?

Put the day and date at the top, then open with « Cher journal, » or an exclamation such as « Quelle journée ! ». Keep it informal throughout, because you are writing to yourself.
