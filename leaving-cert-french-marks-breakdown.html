How Many Marks Is Leaving Cert French Worth?

A full breakdown of oral, aural and written marks at Higher and Ordinary Level — and what it means for how you should study.

Leaving Cert French is worth 400 marks in total, split across three components: the written exam (55%, 220 marks), the oral exam (25%, 100 marks), and the aural/listening exam (20%, 80 marks). Written skills carry the most weight overall, but oral and aural together still make up 45% of your final grade — so neither can be neglected.

The Oral Exam — 25% (100 marks)

The oral exam is a conversation lasting around 12–15 minutes, conducted by a State Examiner. It's one of the more approachable places to pick up marks, since you have real influence over how the conversation unfolds — you can steer it toward topics you've prepared thoroughly.

The Aural (Listening) Exam — 20% (80 marks)

The aural exam takes place after the written paper on exam day. At Higher Level, most sections are played three times, with the final section played twice. It rewards precision — writing down exact details as you hear them — over general comprehension alone.

The Written Exam — 55% (220 marks)

The written exam is the single largest component, combining reading comprehension and written production (essays, opinion pieces, functional writing). Because it carries more than half the total marks, strong written technique — structure, accuracy, and range of vocabulary — has an outsized effect on your final grade.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the French oral worth in the Leaving Cert?

The oral exam is worth 25% of the total mark — 100 marks out of 400. It's conducted as a conversation lasting 12 to 15 minutes with a State Examiner.

How many marks is the French aural or listening exam worth?

The aural exam is worth 20% of the total mark — 80 marks out of 400. It takes place after the written paper on exam day.

How many marks is the French written exam worth?

The written exam, covering reading comprehension and written production, is worth 55% of the total mark — 220 marks out of 400, making it the largest single component.

Is the marks breakdown the same for Ordinary Level French?

Yes. Ordinary Level follows the same overall weighting as Higher Level — 55% written, 25% oral, 20% aural — applied to the Ordinary Level total.
